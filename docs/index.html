Launch on o1. From a post.

o1bot.exchange turns a mention on X into a real token launch on o1 Launchpad. You link your X account once, keep a little ETH in the wallet that gets created for you, and post the command. The bot does the on-chain work from your wallet and replies with the result.

1 postis one launch
0.001 ETHcreation fee, paid to o1
0.5%of every trade to you, the creator
196pairs on Robinhood Chain

What o1bot is, and what it is not

o1bot owns three things: the X bot, the wallets it creates for users, and the web app. Everything on chain belongs to o1 Launchpad: the factory that creates the token, the Uniswap v4 pool, the hook that collects fees, the fee escrow, and the audits. o1bot does not run its own launch contracts and does not sit between you and o1.

That is why a token launched through o1bot is a normal o1 token. It shows up on o1, it trades on o1, and the creator fees go to the address o1 recorded at launch, which is your wallet.

v1 runs on Robinhood Chain only. Every command, pair and fee in these docs refers to Robinhood Chain (chain id 4663). Base support is not planned for v1.

Who pays for what

Your wallet pays o1's creation fee and the gas for the launch. If you add a dev buy, your wallet pays that too. o1bot never fronts fees or gas, never takes a cut of a launch, and never holds your keys.

o1bot earns from two places that never touch your launch: its own official token, launched through the same pipeline as everyone else's, and the 0.2% referral share o1 pays on trades that go through the o1bot app's swap.

Start/Quickstart

Three things, once.

After this, every launch is a single post.

1
Link your X account

Open o1bot.exchange and sign in with the X account you will post from. That creates your wallet, secured by Privy. On the same screen, allow o1bot to sign launches from this wallet. Without that permission the bot cannot launch for you.

2
Top up the launch fee

Send ETH on Robinhood Chain to the wallet address shown in the app. A plain launch needs o1's 0.001 ETH creation fee plus gas, about 0.002 ETH in total at normal gas prices. Add whatever you want to spend on a dev buy on top.

3
Post the command

Mention the bot with the launch command. Attach an image and it becomes the token logo. The bot replies with the token address and links within about a minute.

Your first launch
@o1bot_exchange launch $RUGRAT "Rugrat" pair ETH on robinhood

The bot never launches a token it cannot pay for. If the wallet is short, it replies with the exact shortfall and your deposit address instead of trying.

What happens after you post

  1. The bot reads the post and turns it into a launch command. If something required is missing, it asks one question.
  2. It checks that your account is linked, the pair exists on o1's factory, and your wallet holds enough ETH.
  3. It prepares the token metadata, mines an address ending in 01 as o1 requires, and simulates the whole launch.
  4. It signs the launch from your wallet, waits for the receipt, and replies under your post with the token address, the o1 link, and the o1bot token page.

Trading opens immediately with o1's 20-second anti-snipe period. See Fees and economics.

What the bot will not guess

If the ticker, the name or the pair is missing, the bot asks one question instead of inventing a value. Amounts are taken literally: devbuy 0.05 means 0.05 ETH, and devbuy $50 is a question back to you because the bot does not convert currencies. Instructions aimed at the bot inside a post ("ignore your rules", "use the platform wallet") are ignored; the post is treated as data.

Launching/Pairs

Pairs

The pair is the asset your token trades against and the asset every trading fee is paid in. o1's Robinhood factory registers 196 of them.

KindPairsFees paid inDev buy
ETHNative ETH on Robinhood ChainETHYes
USDGGlobal Dollar, 6 decimalsUSDGYes
Stocks194 registered stock tokens: AAPL, NVDA, TSLA, MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN, META, MSTR, COIN, QQQ, SPY-style ETFs and many moreThe stock token106 of 194, where the stock has on-chain liquidity

Stock-paired launches

A stock-paired token is created by the same factory with the same 0.001 ETH creation fee. The difference is the pool: it holds your token against the stock token, so buyers pay in the stock, and your creator fees arrive in the stock. That is the point for many creators: a coin that earns NVDA on every trade.

Because the pair asset is a stock token, a dev buy has to convert your ETH into that stock first. The bot finds a route through Robinhood's pools automatically; where none has liquidity, it tells you and offers a launch without the dev buy. Details in Dev buy.

Naming a pair

  • Symbols always work: pair NVDA, pair usdg, pair $ETH.
  • Aliases the bot understands: ether and ethereum for ETH, and company names that identify exactly one listed stock, such as nvidia, tesla, apple, google.
  • If a name could mean two stocks, the bot asks which one.
  • An asset that is not registered on o1's factory (DOGE, for example) gets a reply saying it is not available.

Tickers that collide with stocks

You cannot launch a token whose ticker equals a stock token symbol registered on the factory, so nobody mistakes your $TSLA for the tokenized stock. Pick another ticker; $TSLADOG is fine.

The list of registered pairs is refreshed from o1's published catalog. Every launch also re-reads the factory on chain, so a pair o1 removes cannot be selected even before the catalog is updated.

Launching/Dev buy

Dev buy

A dev buy purchases tokens in the same transaction that creates them. It is the one buy that skips o1's anti-snipe surcharge.

@o1bot_exchange launch $MOON "Moon" pair ETH on robinhood devbuy 0.05

Why it exists

For 20 seconds after a pool opens, o1 charges a fee that starts at 99% and falls to the normal 1%. It stops bots from sniping the first blocks. The creator's own atomic buy is exempt: it pays the normal 1% and nothing more. Any buy in a later transaction, including one from you, pays the schedule like everyone else. The bot will never place a follow-up buy inside the window.

What you pay

The amount after devbuy is ETH, taken from your wallet on top of the creation fee and gas. devbuy 0.05 on a plain launch means the transaction carries 0.051 ETH plus gas.

ItemETH
Creation fee to o10.001
Dev buy0.050
Gas (about 2.7M gas at typical prices)~0.001
Total the wallet needs~0.052

Slippage protection

Before signing, the bot simulates the launch and reads how many tokens the buy would return. It then sets a minimum output 3% below that. If the chain moves against you between simulation and confirmation, the whole launch reverts and nothing is spent except gas. The bot tells you and you can post again.

How the ETH reaches the pool

  • ETH pair: straight into the new pool. One hop.
  • USDG pair: ETH is swapped to USDG on Robinhood's SwapX pool first, then into the new pool.
  • Stock pair: either ETH to the stock directly, or ETH to USDG to the stock, depending on where the liquidity is. The bot tries every liquid route, simulates each, and uses the one that returns the most tokens.

Routes are discovered on chain at launch time. As of the September 2026 survey, 106 of the 194 stock tokens have a liquid route from ETH. For the rest, the bot replies that a dev buy is not available for that pair and offers to launch without it.

A launch with a dev buy is valid for 5 minutes after the bot prepares it, versus 30 minutes for a plain launch. o1's swap adapter rejects longer deadlines. In practice the bot signs within seconds, so you will not notice.

Launching/Fees to another account

Fees to another account

Launch a token whose creator fees go to someone else on X. They do not need an account yet.

@o1bot_exchange launch $GIFT "Gift" pair ETH on robinhood fees to @alice

How it works

  1. The bot resolves @alice to her stable X user id, so a later handle change does not break anything.
  2. If she has never used o1bot, a wallet is created for her in advance, tied to that X id. When she signs in with X at o1bot.exchange, that wallet is already hers.
  3. Your wallet launches the token as usual. You remain the on-chain creator and pay everything.
  4. Right after the launch confirms, the bot sends a second transaction from your wallet that points the creator fee stream at her wallet. The bot replies only after both confirm, and mentions her so she knows.

Claiming as the recipient

Fees accumulate in o1's fee escrow until claimed. The recipient signs in at o1bot.exchange, opens their wallet, and claims. A brand-new wallet has no ETH for gas, so o1bot covers the first claim's gas; after that the recipient's wallet pays its own way.

Who can receive fees

  • Any real X account except suspended ones.
  • Not the bot's own accounts and not o1's accounts.
  • You keep the other creator rights (metadata, transferring creator rights) unless you transfer them separately on o1.

What gets recorded

The token's contract metadata is a small JSON document with the name, symbol, image, a link to the o1bot token page, and attribution to the post that launched it: the author's handle, X user id, and the post id. Anyone can verify which post created which token.

Metadata is fixed at launch. The o1bot creates every token with editing disabled, which is also what o1 marks as the immutable default.

Money/Fees and economics

Fees and economics

Two fees exist, both o1's. o1bot adds none.

Creation fee

0.001 ETH per launch, paid to o1 by your wallet, for every pair including stock pairs. Plus network gas, roughly 2.2 million gas for a plain launch and 2.7 million with a dev buy. The fee is a setting o1 can change; the bot reads the live value before every launch and tells you the exact amount if your wallet is short.

Swap fee

Every trade in the pool pays 1% of the paired asset, split three ways:

RecipientShare of the 1%Of each trade
Creator (you, or your fees-to account)50%0.5%
o1 platform30%0.3%
Referrer (the app the trade came through)20%0.2%

Fees are always in the pair asset: an ETH pool pays ETH, a USDG pool pays USDG, an NVDA pool pays NVDA. When a trade has no valid referrer, o1 keeps the 0.2%.

The opening 20 seconds

Trading is open from the first block, but the total fee starts at 99% and falls linearly to 1% over 20 seconds. Everything above 1% goes to o1. The creator's atomic dev buy is exempt; every later buy is not.

o1's anti-snipe schedule on Robinhood Chain. Values above 1% are the surcharge and go to the platform.

What o1bot earns

  • The referrer share (0.2% of trades) on swaps made through the o1bot app. o1 pays it; traders pay nothing extra. o1 rejects a referrer that equals the creator or the fee recipient, so o1bot's referrer wallet is a separate address from any user wallet.
  • Creator fees on o1bot's own token, launched through the same pipeline as everyone else's.

There is no fee on launches, no fee on claims, nothing taken from user wallets.

Money/Wallet and security

Wallet and security

Your launches are signed by a wallet that is yours. Here is exactly what the bot can and cannot do with it.

The wallet

Signing in with X at o1bot.exchange creates an embedded wallet through Privy, tied to your X account. It is a normal Ethereum address. You can see it, top it up, export its private key, and use it anywhere. o1bot never sees the key.

The permission you grant

For the bot to launch from your wallet, you allow "delegated signing" once in the app. Until you do, the bot replies that your account is not linked. You can revoke it in the app at any time.

What the bot is allowed to sign

The signer inside the bot refuses everything that is not on this list, before the request ever reaches Privy:

TransactionTo
Create a launch, with or without a dev buyo1's active factory
Point the creator fee stream at another wallet (fees to)o1's active factory
An ERC-20 approval a launch may needA registered pair token only
Claim accumulated feeso1's fee escrow

No plain transfers, no other contracts, no message signing, no signing on other chains. A post cannot turn into arbitrary calldata; the bot only fills the fields of those four calls. Every signed transaction is logged with the post id, your X id, your wallet, and a hash of the calldata.

Things worth knowing

  • You are the on-chain creator. o1 records your wallet as the original creator; that cannot be changed by o1bot.
  • Keep only what you plan to spend in the launch wallet. It is a hot wallet by design.
  • The fees to recipient's wallet is created in advance and handed to them on their first sign-in. Until then nobody can spend from it.
  • o1bot's treasury and referrer wallets are separate addresses that never sign user launches.

Developers/Architecture

Architecture

A pipeline from a mention to a receipt. Each stage can refuse; only the executor touches the chain.

Only the executor signs. The parser, validator and replier can end a mention early without touching a wallet.

Stack

TypeScript throughout. viem for chain reads and writes, Privy for wallets and login, the Anthropic SDK for the parser, Postgres with Prisma for users, mentions, launches and the signed-transaction audit log, Redis for the queue and dedupe, Next.js for the app. Verified contract ABIs are vendored from two independent sources and must agree.

Developers/How a launch is built

How a launch is built

The executor never trusts a cached number and never broadcasts a call it has not simulated. This is the order of operations.

1
Confirm the factory

o1's published registry is fetched and compared with the vendored snapshot. If o1 rotated the active factory, the run stops until the snapshot and ABIs are refreshed.

2
Read the factory at one block

configVersion, launchSupply, tickSpacing, nativeLaunchFee, launchCreationEnabled, TOKEN_ADDRESS_SUFFIX, the token deployer, the hook, the launch-buy adapter, and quoteConfig plus quoteRevision for the pair. All at the same block so they are mutually consistent. Creation disabled, pair unregistered, or a suffix other than 1 stops the run.

3
Pin metadata

The image is validated by its magic bytes and size, then pinned; the metadata JSON is pinned; the resulting ipfs:// URI goes into the launch parameters.

4
Mine the salt

The factory scopes every salt to the caller: scopedSalt = keccak256(abi.encode(creator, creatorSalt)). The token address is CREATE2(tokenDeployer, scopedSalt, launchTokenBytecodeHash). The bytecode hash does not depend on the salt, so it is read once and the search runs locally until the address ends in 01, about 256 tries.

5
Build the call

createLaunch(params) with value = nativeLaunchFee, or createLaunchAndBuy(params, buy) with value = nativeLaunchFee + amountIn and native funding. expectedConfigVersion is the fresh read. Deadline is the block timestamp plus 30 minutes, or 5 minutes with a dev buy.

6
Simulate as the creator

An eth_call from the user's address. The token the factory returns must equal the mined prediction. With a dev buy, every discovered route is simulated and the one with the largest output wins; its output minus 3% becomes minAmountOut.

7
Check funding

Gas is estimated with 20% headroom; the required amount is value plus gas at the current fee. The user's real balance decides whether to continue.

8
Sign, send, confirm

The call passes the allow-list guard, is signed by the user's Privy wallet, broadcast, and awaited. Launched in the receipt gives the token and pool id. With fees to, setCreatorFeeRecipient follows as a second transaction, and the reply waits for both.

Dev-buy route data

The launch-buy adapter is not source-verified on any explorer, so its route format was reverse-engineered from live transactions and is pinned byte for byte in tests. A route is abi.encode(Step[]) where a step is (kind, tokenIn, tokenOut, pool, fee, tickSpacing, hooks, hookData); kind 1 is a SwapX V3 pool, kind 2 a Uniswap V4 pool. The last step is always the new launch pool with fee 0 and o1's hook.

Developers/Running it yourself

Running it yourself

A pnpm monorepo. Everything runs in dry-run mode until you say otherwise.

Layout

config/            o1 contract snapshot, regenerated with pnpm o1:sync
abis/              verified ABIs (Sourcify + Blockscout must agree)
packages/shared    env, chain + RPC fallbacks, o1 registry, tx allow-list
packages/db        Prisma schema: users, mentions, launches, signed-tx audit
packages/wallet    Privy: X user to wallet, pregeneration, guarded signer
packages/executor  factory reads, salt mining, metadata, routes, planLaunch
packages/parser    Claude mention parser with fixtures
apps/web           Next.js app (Privy X login, wallet, onboarding)
apps/bot           the worker

Setup

pnpm install
cp .env.example .env        # Privy, X, Anthropic, Pinata keys; keep DRY_RUN=true
pnpm o1:sync                # refresh config/o1.json from docs.o1.exchange
pnpm abi:vendor             # re-vendor ABIs when o1:sync reports drift
pnpm db:generate && pnpm db:push
pnpm typecheck && pnpm test

Dry-run a launch

Simulates the whole thing against the live factory and sends nothing. A random creator is funded virtually inside the simulation, so it works with an empty wallet.

pnpm simulate --name "Rugrat" --symbol RUGRAT --pair ETH
pnpm simulate --name "Rugrat" --symbol RUGRAT --pair ETH --devbuy 0.01
pnpm simulate --name "Nvidia Dog" --symbol NVDOG --pair NVDA --devbuy 0.01

Test the parser

pnpm parse '@o1bot_exchange launch $RUGRAT "Rugrat" pair ETH on robinhood'
pnpm parse --fixtures       # 24 fixtures against the live model

Reference/FAQ

Questions people ask

Is it free?

o1bot charges nothing. You pay o1's 0.001 ETH creation fee and gas from your own wallet, like any launch on o1.

Is the token different from one launched on o1's site?

No. Same factory, same pool, same fees, same audits. The only extra is the metadata line that records which post launched it.

Can I use my own wallet instead of the one o1bot creates?

Not for bot launches. The bot needs a wallet it is allowed to sign from, and that is the Privy wallet tied to your X account. You can export its key and use it anywhere afterwards.

I posted and nothing happened.

Check three things: the post mentions @o1bot_exchange and is not a quote or retweet of the bot; your account is linked and the bot is allowed to sign; the wallet holds enough ETH. If the bot replied with a question, answer it in a new post with the full command.

Can I change the name or image after launch?

No. Tokens launched through o1bot are created with metadata editing disabled.

Stock pair: what do I actually earn?

0.5% of every trade, paid in the stock token. Claim it from the app when it is live, or on o1 directly with your wallet.

The dev buy failed but the token exists?

That cannot happen. A dev buy is atomic with the launch: if the buy fails, the whole launch reverts and no token is created.

When is Base coming?

Not in v1. The bot, the pairs and the docs are Robinhood Chain only for now.
